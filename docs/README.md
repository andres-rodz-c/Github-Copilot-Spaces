# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

## Overview

OctoAcme uses a customer-first, iterative project management approach with clear ownership, measurable outcomes, and continuous learning. Projects move through initiation, planning, execution and tracking, release and deployment, and retrospective and continuous improvement. Throughout the lifecycle, teams manage risks and dependencies, communicate with stakeholders, validate quality through testing and reviews, and capture decisions and action items.

During **initiation**, the team validates the business need, defines a measurable goal and success criteria, identifies stakeholders, outlines the timeline and risks, and decides whether the work should move into planning. In **planning**, an approved initiative becomes an actionable delivery plan: the team creates and prioritizes a backlog, defines acceptance criteria and the Definition of Done, estimates work, identifies dependencies, and maps milestones and releases.

During **execution and tracking**, teams use a project board to manage work from Backlog through Done, supported by small pull requests that link to issues and acceptance criteria. Project Managers coordinate delivery, schedules, risks, resources, and communication; Product Managers define outcomes and priorities; Developers build and test solutions; QA validates quality and acceptance criteria; and stakeholders provide input and approvals. Daily standups, weekly delivery or PM syncs, sprint or milestone demos, and stakeholder updates maintain alignment. Risks are recorded in a register, reviewed regularly, and escalated from the team to the Project Manager, Product Lead, and sponsor when needed.

Quality assurance is continuous: teams use unit, integration, and critical-flow end-to-end tests, manual QA where appropriate, CI checks, linting, security scans, code review, and staging smoke tests. Before **release and deployment**, acceptance criteria must be met, release notes and rollback plans prepared, and post-deployment verification completed. After each sprint, release, milestone, or incident, **retrospectives and continuous improvement** capture what went well, identify improvements, assign measurable action items, and review progress in subsequent syncs.
